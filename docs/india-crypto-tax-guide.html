The two rules that matter

India taxes gains from Virtual Digital Assets (VDAs — a category that includes cryptocurrency) under two separate provisions of the Income Tax Act:

1. Flat 30% tax on gains — Section 115BBH

Any net profit from transferring a VDA is taxed at a flat 30%, with no lower slab for smaller gains and no deduction allowed other than the cost of acquiring the asset itself. Two consequences that surprise people:

  • Losses from one VDA transaction cannot be set off against gains from another VDA transaction, or against any other type of income (salary, business income, etc.).
  • VDA losses cannot be carried forward to a future financial year.

2. 1% TDS — Section 194S

The exchange (or, in some peer-to-peer cases, the buyer) withholds 1% of the full trade value as TDS at the time of the transaction — not 1% of the profit. This applies whether that particular trade turns out to be a gain or a loss. TDS withheld through the year is credited against your actual 30% tax liability when you file, and refunded if it exceeds what you actually owe.

Common misconceptions

  1. "I don't owe tax unless I withdraw to my bank account." Not true — the taxable event is the transfer itself (a sale or a swap), not the later act of moving funds to a bank.
  2. "I can offset crypto losses against my other income." Not true — VDA losses can only ever offset VDA losses, and even that is disallowed under Section 115BBH; they cannot reduce your salary or business income, and cannot be carried forward.
  3. "The 1% TDS is my total tax bill." Not true — TDS is an upfront withholding on trade value. Your actual liability is 30% of net gains, reconciled separately at filing.
  4. "Swapping one coin for another isn't taxable since I never touched INR." Not true — a crypto-to-crypto trade is still a transfer under the law, and triggers both the 30% tax on any gain and the 1% TDS.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a tax-free threshold for crypto gains in India?
No — unlike some other capital gains categories, Section 115BBH applies the flat 30% rate to gains of any size, from the very first rupee of net profit.

Does the 30% rate apply to short-term or long-term holdings?
The distinction doesn't apply to VDAs the way it does to stocks or property — the flat 30% rate is the same regardless of how long you held the asset before transferring it.

Do I still need to report crypto activity if TDS was already withheld?
Generally yes — TDS withholding and your income tax filing are separate obligations. This page doesn't cover filing mechanics; talk to a tax professional about your specific reporting requirements.
